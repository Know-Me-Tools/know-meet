Three marks for Know Meet

Selected: A · Gathering. See the brand guide for the final mark and its rules.

Each concept is built from what the product actually is: the KnowMe ember dot, persistent dots and agents sitting in the meeting, shared versus private conversation, and the video grid. Every mark is shown on dark and light, as a lockup, an app icon, at favicon sizes and in one colour, so you can judge it where it will really live.

Gathering

An ember core — the meeting’s shared memory — with five participants around it. One of them is cyan: an agent sitting among the people.

What it says

The centre is the same ember dot that sits at the heart of the KnowMe K. Here it becomes the thing everyone gathers around: the meeting’s knowledge, which every person, dot and agent in the room can draw on.

Tie to KnowMe

It is a direct descendant of the parent mark, so Know Meet reads as part of the KnowMe family without needing the K.

Works well

  • The closest family tie to KnowMe.
  • Tells the “people and agents together” story in one shape.
  • Animates naturally: the ring can breathe or pulse to show a dot listening, thinking or speaking.

Watch out for

  • Dot clusters are common in collaboration and AI branding, so it needs the wordmark early on.
  • It does not say “video” on its own.
  • At 16 px the five outer dots merge slightly; still recognisable as a ring.

What it says

Each arch is a person, or a conversation: the room’s group chat and a private one-to-one, people and agents. The shared leg and the dot below it are where they connect.

Tie to KnowMe

It borrows the rounded, heavy strokes of the KnowMe K’s arms and keeps the ember dot as the meeting point, so it sits alongside the parent mark as a sibling monogram.

Works well

  • The most ownable shape of the three: a monogram no other meeting app uses.
  • Works as a letter, so it pairs well with the wordmark and in tight UI spaces.
  • Calm and confident; suits a product meant for serious work meetings.

Watch out for

  • Says less about agents and dots than A or C; that story has to come from elsewhere.
  • Built from strokes, so the final version needs outlining for production.
  • At 16 px the arches thicken; the dot stays visible.

What it says

This is the product’s difference stated plainly: a normal video meeting, except one of the participants is an agent or a persistent dot. The open tile is the same shape the room uses for agent tiles.

Tie to KnowMe

The tie to KnowMe is the ember dot alone, so it relies on colour and the wordmark more than A does.

Works well

  • Instantly reads as a video meeting, even to someone who has never heard of Know Meet.
  • The crispest of the three at 16 px.
  • Maps directly onto the product UI: the logo is literally the room.

Watch out for

  • Grid icons are close to other conferencing apps, so the ember dot has to carry the distinction.
  • The weakest visual link to the KnowMe K.
  • A literal icon can feel less like a brand mark over time.
